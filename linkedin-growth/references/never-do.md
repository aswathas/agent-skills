# Never do

These are the habits that quietly cap an account. None of them produce an error;
all of them cost reach or trust.

## Reach killers

1. **Engagement pods.** Coordinated liking and commenting is detectable and
   LinkedIn suppresses it. If a comment exists only to be seen by the author,
   skip it.
2. **"Comment YES and I'll send it."** Bait. The feed learns the post is a
   trick and the audience learns you are selling something.
3. **Posting a bare link.** The feed prefers posts that keep people on LinkedIn.
   Put the link at the end, or in the first comment.
4. **Editing the post in the first hour.** It can reset distribution. Proofread
   before publishing.
5. **Deleting and reposting a flop.** It rarely does better the second time and
   it trains you to chase the feed instead of the work.
6. **More than 5 hashtags.** Six starts to read as spam. Three to five, all
   relevant.
7. **Posting and ghosting.** No reply for hours after posting is the single most
   common self-inflicted wound. Stay 30 minutes.

## Trust killers

8. **Invented numbers.** One rounded-up metric that someone can disprove costs
   more than the metric was worth.
9. **Fake case studies.** "A client made $40k" with no client, no proof, and no
   permission.
10. **Claiming credit that is not yours.** If a maintainer wrote the merged
    commit, say so. If a teammate built half of it, name them.
11. **Tagging people who did not contribute.** It gets attention once and
    reputational damage every time after.
12. **AI tells.** "Game-changer", "seamless", "in today's fast-paced world".
    Readers pattern-match these in under a second.
13. **Motivational arcs with no event.** "I failed. Here's what I learned."
    without the failure, the number, or the fix.

## Format killers

14. **The wall.** Ten lines with no break. People read on phones.
15. **The teaser hook.** "Here's what nobody tells you about X" followed by a
    line break and no substance.
16. **The screenshot of text.** If the content is worth a screenshot, it is worth
    writing properly.
17. **Numbered lists of 10 where 3 are real.** Padding is visible.

## Judgment calls

- **Politics and outrage.** Not a growth strategy for a builder account; it
  attracts an audience that does not buy or hire.
- **Competitor teardowns that name and shame.** They travel, then they follow
  you into every interview.
- **Personal news with no professional hook.** Once or twice a year is human;
  more is a diary.
- **Reposting someone else's work without credit.** Always credit, always link.

## The one rule that covers the rest

If the post would still be true and still be useful with your name removed, it
is not your post. Publish the version only you could write.
